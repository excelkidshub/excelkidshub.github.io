10 Phonics Activities for Kids at Home — Ages 4–7

Build your child's reading skills in just 10 minutes a day — with activities that feel like play, not work.

The best phonics learning does not only happen in class. Ten minutes of daily phonics practice at home — when done consistently — can dramatically accelerate your child's reading progress. Here are ten activities that are simple, effective, and enjoyable for children aged 4–7.

Note: These activities work best alongside structured phonics instruction. They are not a replacement for a qualified teacher — but as daily reinforcement between sessions, they are extremely powerful.

1. Sound Spotting Walk

On your next walk — to school, the park, or the market — pick a letter sound and look for things that start with that sound. "How many things can you find that start with /s/?" Children love this game and it builds phonemic awareness effortlessly.

Age: 3–6 | Time: As long as the walk

2. Oral Blending Game

Say a word in its individual sounds and ask your child to blend it: "What word is this? /c/-/a/-/t/?" Start with 3-letter CVC words (consonant-vowel-consonant) and gradually increase complexity. No materials needed — this can be done anywhere.

Age: 4–7 | Time: 5 minutes

3. Letter Sound Sorting

Collect 10–15 small household objects or picture cards and sort them by their initial sound. "Which of these start with /b/? Which start with /m/?" This builds both phonemic awareness and letter-sound correspondence.

Age: 3–5 | Time: 10 minutes

4. Magnetic Letters on the Fridge

Magnetic letters are one of the best phonics investments. Ask your child to build simple words: "Can you make the word 'pin'?" Children can feel the letters with their hands as they sound them out — multi-sensory learning at home.

Age: 4–7 | Time: 5–10 minutes

5. Say-It-Slowly Segmenting

Ask your child to spell a word by saying each sound slowly: "Let's stretch out the word 'dog' — what sounds can you hear? /d/-/o/-/g/." Use fingers to count the sounds. This builds the segmenting skill that underpins accurate spelling.

Age: 4–7 | Time: 5 minutes

6. Read Together Pointing

When reading a bedtime story, point to each word as you read it. For familiar pages, ask your child to point while you read. Then try a page together where they point and attempt to sound out simple words. Even 5 minutes of this each night builds enormous reading skill over weeks.

Age: 3–7 | Time: 5–15 minutes

7. The Rhyming Game

Rhyming is a key phonemic awareness skill. Play rhyming games during car journeys or meals: "What rhymes with 'cat'? What rhymes with 'pin'?" Challenge older children to give three rhyming words instead of one.

Age: 3–5 | Time: Any spare moment

8. Salt or Sand Tray Writing

Pour salt or fine sand into a tray and ask your child to write letters in it with their finger. This adds a tactile, sensory dimension to letter formation practice — particularly good for children who resist workbook writing.

Age: 3–6 | Time: 5–10 minutes

9. Word Family Ladders

Pick a word ending (called a "rime") like "-at" and build a word family ladder together: cat, bat, hat, mat, sat, fat, rat. Write each word as you say it. This builds pattern recognition — a key skill for reading speed and spelling.

Age: 5–7 | Time: 5 minutes

10. Phonics Bingo

Draw a 3x3 grid and write a letter sound in each square. Call out words and ask your child to mark the letter their word starts with (or ends with, for a challenge). First to get a row wins. Children play this repeatedly without getting bored.

Age: 4–7 | Time: 10–15 minutes

A Note on Consistency

The key to these activities is regularity, not intensity. Ten minutes every day produces far better results than one hour once a week. Build one or two of these activities into your daily routine — before school, after school, or at bedtime — and you will see a measurable difference in your child's reading within 4–6 weeks.
