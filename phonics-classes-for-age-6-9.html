Why Many 6–9 Year Olds Struggle with Reading

Children in Class 1–4 who struggle with English reading are often intelligent, capable learners — but they have gaps in phonics knowledge that school has not filled. Common signs include:

  • Reading slowly and hesitantly — sounding out every letter
  • Guessing words from pictures or context instead of reading them
  • Making frequent spelling mistakes — even for common words
  • Avoiding reading aloud in class — embarrassment from struggling
  • Performing below their true ability in English exams

These are not signs of low ability. They are signs of a missing foundation. Structured phonics fills those gaps — often rapidly.

Frequently Asked Questions

My child is in Class 3 — is it too late?

Not at all. Children aged 6–9 respond very well to targeted phonics. At this age, children already have vocabulary and language — adding the phonics system to that foundation produces fast results.

Will this help with my child's school exams?

Yes. Stronger phonics leads directly to better spelling accuracy, improved reading comprehension, and greater confidence in English — all of which impact exam performance.

My child knows the alphabet — do they still need phonics?

Knowing letter names is not the same as knowing letter sounds. Phonics teaches the 42 sounds of English (many letters have multiple sounds, and there are letter combinations like "ch" and "sh"). Many children who "know the alphabet" still have significant gaps in phonics knowledge.

How quickly will I see improvement?

Most parents notice a difference within 6–8 weeks for children in this age group. Significant reading fluency usually develops within 3–4 months.
